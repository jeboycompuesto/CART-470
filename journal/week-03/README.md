## What I worked on

This week involved a lot of research and meetings. My focus was on understanding the direction of the Soundscape Explorer project and gathering references that could help us turn the ideas into something concrete. I added the client-meeting notes and research links to our Fizzy board so the team could return to them as we develop the project.

The September 23 discussion with Florian Grond helped clarify the experience we are working toward: an interactive campus map where someone can select a location and explore its soundscape, with visual material such as a panorama. The meeting also raised questions about the recording workflow, web playback, and how the different assets would fit together. These are useful starting points for deciding what our first prototype should demonstrate.

## Research and contributions

I gathered references for IAMF, Nerfstudio, and textile surfaces, and shared them in the board's Links column. These references open up different directions for the project:

- **IAMF:** a starting point for investigating immersive audio and the playback questions raised in the meeting.
- **Nerfstudio:** a reference to investigate when considering how we might represent an environment visually.
- **Surface research:** an exploratory direction alongside the meeting's discussion of tactile interaction and accessibility.

At this stage, these are research leads. The next step is to establish which ones actually support the experience we want to build. Adding a resource to the board gives us a shared reference, but we still need practical tests before treating it as a technical solution.

## Reflection on the process

The main shift this week is moving from collecting possibilities toward deciding what to make. Research and meetings have given us more direction, but they have also exposed how many parts need to work together: a location on the map, its audio, its visual material, and the interaction that connects them.

A small prototype would help us evaluate those connections. The meeting proposed starting with outdoor locations and simple stereo recordings. That gives us a manageable way to explore the recording-to-map workflow before tackling the more demanding immersive-audio goals. It also gives the team something concrete to discuss and improve.

One question I want to keep in view is how the interface can make listening central to the experience. A visually interesting environment is only useful here if it helps someone explore and understand the soundscape. The accessibility discussion is another reason to think carefully about how people navigate the project and what information each interaction provides.

## Next steps

My next focus is turning the research and meeting ideas into something we can test with the team:

1. Help define the smallest useful prototype: selecting a location and accessing its associated sound and visual material.
2. Follow up on the proposed outdoor recording exercise and clarify how audio, images, and location information should be organized.
3. Investigate playback options and document what still needs testing before we choose the technology.
4. Turn the research leads into specific Fizzy tasks, with clear responsibilities and outcomes.
