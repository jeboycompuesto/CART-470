# Week 7 — Connecting accessibility research to the prototype

**Jeboy Compuesto · CART 470 · November 4, 2026**  
**Project: Concordia Soundscape Explorer**

[Back to journal](../../README.md)

As we reach the midterm stage, my focus is on how the UI can bring our project's different parts together. My role is centered on the Figma interface and the experience of choosing a campus location, opening its panorama, and listening to its soundscape. The research documented in my previous entry gives me a foundation for thinking through those interactions.

One question I am carrying forward is how users will understand what they can do inside the experience. Having immersive audio available does not automatically make the interface understandable. The design needs to communicate how to start listening, change the viewing direction, select another location, and return to the map. These interactions also need to fit the audio functionality the team is developing.

Accessibility remains an important part of my direction. During our October 21 client meeting, I raised screen-reader support and asked about opportunities to test with blind users. The discussion about a list alternative to the map is particularly relevant to my UI work. A location list could provide another way to browse campus recordings, while keyboard controls and clear labels could make the experience less dependent on using a mouse.

My earlier references help me think about different aspects of the interface. Cities and Memory and Radio Aporee connect recordings with places, while HOAST demonstrates immersive viewing and listening. Audiokinetic's 3D Meter article also provides a reference for showing sound direction visually. However, I see that visualization as a possible enhancement; it would still need accessible alternatives.

The main challenge is keeping the scope manageable while making the prototype feel connected. I need to coordinate with the team so my design work supports what we can implement, rather than developing interface ideas separately from the technical work. At this stage, a clear experience for one location would be a useful foundation for expanding the project.

My next steps are to develop the location-selection and soundscape screens in Figma, review the controls with the teammates handling maps and audio, and identify which accessibility features we can include in the prototype. I also want to keep the possibility of user testing in view so that our design decisions can eventually be informed by people using the experience.
