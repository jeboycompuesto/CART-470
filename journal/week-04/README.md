## What I worked on

Last week, my entry focused on research and the direction established through our client meeting. This week, we recorded audio around school, and I also took panoramic photographs. This moved the project toward gathering real material for the Soundscape Explorer. I participated in client meetings and team meetings, and I added material to our shared Figma workspace.

On October 6, I uploaded the materials through the **Audio Files** and **Panoramic Photos** cards on Fizzy so the team could access them in one place. My contribution covered both capturing visual material and sharing the assets we need for the prototype.

The Audio Files card contains five WAV files labelled EV2, EV, Library, JMSB, and Tunnel, with September 30 dates in their filenames. It also includes photographs, JSON metadata files, and additional recording files. The Panoramic Photos card contains five JPEGs, from `IMG_0004.jpeg` through `IMG_0008.jpeg`. Sharing these materials gives the team actual assets to work with as we develop the Soundscape Explorer.

## Reflection on the process

Having these files available makes the next stage more concrete. We can start asking how a particular recording and image will be connected to a location, instead of only discussing possible technologies. This follows the direction of my previous entry: move toward a small example that brings a place, its sound, and its visual context together.

An important issue at this stage is how the assets are organized. The audio filenames include location labels, while the panoramic images have general numbered filenames. Before integration, we need to confirm which image belongs with each recording and give those relationships clear labels. Otherwise, the team could spend time guessing how the files fit together. The metadata files are also something to inspect when deciding what information each location needs.

File size is another consideration for the prototype. The five panoramic images on the board range from about 16 to 28 MB each. We should test how they load in a browser and whether smaller copies would be appropriate, while retaining the originals. Having the files collected is useful progress, but their availability alone does not establish how well the final experience will run.

Alongside recording and photography, our client and team meetings remained part of the development process. I also contributed to the shared Figma workspace. The FigJam board now brings research, audio, photographs, and a proposed web stack into the same space, making the connections between these parts easier to discuss.

In Discord, André shared that he was working through the proposed pipeline in Figma. I offered to take a look and help with using Figma. This is a useful way for me to contribute alongside gathering media: helping the team communicate ideas visually as we work out how the experience will fit together. The diagram is still a plan, and the next challenge is translating it into a small working example.

## Next steps

William added references for a JsAmbisonics demo and the Veri 360-degree JavaScript library to the board. These give us possible starting points to investigate alongside the assets. Choosing a reference is only one step; we still need to test how a viewer and audio playback fit our project.

The next useful milestone is a single location that the team can open and evaluate. To get there, the priorities are:

1. Match a recording with the correct photograph or panorama and label the location clearly.
2. Check the associated metadata and agree on a consistent way to organize the files.
3. Test that small set of assets in a browser, including loading and playback.
4. Document the results and use them to decide what to improve before adding more locations.

This week's contribution gives us a shared collection to build from. The next step is making one part of that collection work as a connected experience.
